# Evri Tracking Number: 16-Digit & Calling Card Explained

Evri gives every parcel that travels through its UK network its own tracking reference. Using the wrong ID (for example an order number alone) is the most common reason a lookup fails.

## The 16-character parcel tracking code

Most UK Evri parcels use a **16-character** code — letters and numbers — shared by the retailer or shown in dispatch emails/SMS.

You’ll usually find it:

- In the dispatch email from your retailer  
- In Evri delivery notifications  
- On the postage label if you sent the parcel yourself  

Copy the **entire** string into the tracker on [everitracking.com](/).

## The 8-digit calling card number

If your courier tried to deliver and you were out, they may leave a calling card with an **8-digit** number. Enter that number in the tracker to see the next steps for your parcel.

## ParcelShop receipt numbers

Drop-offs can show a ParcelShop receipt (often starting with **P** plus digits). Keep the receipt until tracking shows the parcel accepted into the network.

## What not to use

- Marketplace order IDs without the Evri tracking field  
- Partial codes missing leading letters  
- Invoice or payment references  

Only the tracking number (or calling card) works reliably on this site.

## Where to find it on popular retailers

Open your order confirmation or “your parcel is on its way” message. Look for **Evri**, older **Hermes** branding in some emails, or **tracking number**. If you cannot find it, contact the sender — they booked the label.
